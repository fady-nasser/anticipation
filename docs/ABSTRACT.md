# Anticipation in Football: From Predicting Opponents to Understanding Teammates

**Authors:** Fady Nasser, et al.  
**Track:** Research Paper Submission Track  
**Conference:** MIT Sloan Sports Analytics Conference (SSAC 2027)

---

### Introduction
Anticipation is fundamental to football expertise, enabling players to predict emerging situations and respond before they unfold. Skilled players exploit opponents’ movements, postural cues, and contextual information to anticipate actions (Williams-1998; North-2016). Equally important is anticipating teammates’ intentions, facilitating coordination through shared tactical understanding and reducing the need for explicit communication (Blaser-2019). Celebrated player partnerships, often described as having “telepathic” chemistry, illustrate how familiarity and shared expectations enable precisely timed, coordinated actions (Gershgoren-2016). Yet, anticipation is predominantly assessed through isolated perceptual tasks, offering limited insight into its continuous expression during matches. Player-tracking data provide opportunities to quantify anticipation through off-ball positioning, movement initiation, and spatiotemporal coordination, extending its assessment to observable, context-dependent tactical behaviors.

### Methods
We used the events and player tracking data of the PFF FC 2022 World Cup dataset. For each event, player movement profiles were extracted over a temporal window extending from shortly before the event to the subsequent event. Profiles included directional velocity, speed, and distance to the ball. The ball-motion profile was extracted over the same window. Granger causality analysis was then performed between pairs of player movement profiles to identify significant directional temporal dependencies while incorporating the corresponding ball-motion profile as a control variable to account for movement dependencies driven by ball dynamics. This procedure was applied to both intra-team and inter-team player pairs. Finally, recurrence maps were constructed to characterize the temporal structure of these predictive relationships across the game, separately representing teammate-to-teammate and player-to-opponent anticipation. Beside reported results in the literature (Benhida-2025), and due to the lack of standard reference, our results were compared against experts’ tactical analyses (FIFA Training Centre, Total Football Analysis, Coaches' Voice) documenting evidence of unusually strong on-field coordination during the tournament.

### Results
Figure 1 shows within-team anticipation maps for four teams with distinct playing styles—France, Argentina, Morocco, and Serbia—revealing different tactical hierarchies. In France, Tchouaméni and Rabiot formed the squad's strongest reciprocal link, each triggering the other's runs 293 and 275 times (Tchouaméni&Rabiot). In Argentina, Mac Allister and Messi exchanged 218 anticipation phases (Messi&Allister), while Álvarez repeatedly Granger-caused Messi's movement into vacated attacking pockets across 169 phases (Alvarez&Messi). In Morocco, Ziyech and Hakimi displayed near-perfect reciprocity, each triggering the other's runs in 187 and 183 phases (Ziyech&Hakimi, Benhida-2025). In Serbia, Tadić's Granger-causation of Mitrović's box runs stood as the team's top partnership, with 215 and 206 phases, respectively (Tadić&Mitrović). Figure 2 illustrates the mutual anticipation maps for France and Argentina teams in the final match, showing MacAllister was well anticipating Griezmann (Final-Report1), and Tchouaméni and Griezmann anticipating Enzo Fernández and Di María as Argentina's central midfielders and attackers (Final-Report2, Final-Report3).

### Conclusion
By distinguishing anticipation between teammates and opponents and estimating both its direction and temporal lag, the proposed approach provides a novel way to characterize individual and interpersonal anticipation during match play. The resulting maps offer a compact representation of these relationships, with potential applications in evaluating player intelligence, team coordination, scouting, and tactical analysis.
